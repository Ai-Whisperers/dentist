**Research Date:** June 2026

## 1. Habilitación Sanitaria (Health Ministry Permit)

### Legal Basis
- **Ley 836/1980** — Código Sanitario (governs all health establishment habilitation)
- **Decreto 22.439/1998** — Regulations for the practice of odontology in Paraguay
- **Resolución S.G. N° 106/2013** — Mandatory habilitation for dental offices, clinics, and dental prosthesis labs; governs fees
- **Resolución S.G. N° 316/2023** — Approves the catalog of clinical specialties for the odontology career at MSPBS

### Which Authority Handles It
The **Dirección de Establecimientos de Salud, Afines y Tecnología Sanitaria** (within MSPBS) handles habilitation. For dental-specific issues, the **Dirección de Salud Bucodental** (DSBD) is the relevant sub-directorate.

### Types of Dental Establishments Requiring Habilitation
1. **Consultorio odontológico** — Solo or small group dental practice
2. **Clínica odontológica** — More complex dental clinic
3. **Clínica odontológica con área de enseñanza** — Dental clinic with teaching area
4. **Laboratorio de prótesis dental** — Dental prosthesis laboratory

### Steps to Get Licensed

1. **Register the professional** with the MSPBS — must have a current Registro Profesional (professional registration) issued by the Ministry
2. **Prepare architectural plans** (scale 1:50 for spaces up to 1,000 m²; 1:100 for larger) showing all rooms, measurements, fixed equipment
3. **Obtain a Certificate of Approval of Plans** from the relevant municipal or governmental body
4. **Submit the habilitation application** to the Dirección de Establecimientos de Salud with all required documents
5. **Pay the applicable tariff** per the fee schedule in Resolución S.G. N° 106/2013 (aranceles)
6. **Pass inspection** by the habilitation authority
7. **Receive the Habilitación Certificate** — required to operate

### Documentary Requirements (General)
- Completed application form
- Sworn declaration (declaración jurada) of data
- Building plans (2 original copies)
- Site/location plan (scale 1:500)
- Copy of professional registration and cédula of the regente (responsible dentist)
- Patent or title of the responsible professional
- Tax identification (RUC)
- Staff list (nómina del personal)
- Inventory of furniture and equipment signed by owner and regente
- For renewals: Sworn declaration that no modifications have been made + prior habilitación copy

### Specific to Dental Consultorios
The **Grilla odontológica** (odontological form) is required in addition to the standard documentation, applicable specifically to consultorios, clinical odontológicas, and dental clinics with teaching areas.

### Physical Requirements for a Dental Office (Consultorio Odontológico)

Based on the compiled requirements from MSPBS Dirección de Establecimientos de Salud:

**Minimum area:** Not explicitly stated as a single universal figure for consultorio odontológico in the summary, but the general requirement for a medical consultorio is minimum **9 m²** in plant. Dental operatories typically require more due to equipment.

**General conditions:**
- Reception area / waiting room
- Sanitary facilities (bathroom) with accessibility adaptations for persons with disabilities
- Operatory or dental chair area with minimum dimensions to accommodate the dental equipment, suction, compressed air
- Area for sterilization
- Storage for supplies and equipment

**Bathroom requirements:**
- Must have accessibility adaptations for persons with disabilities (Ley 4.934/2013 de accesibilidad al medio físico)
- Must be connected to running water (cold and hot recommended)
- Floors and walls must be waterproof/impermeable in wet areas

**Sterilization area requirements:**
- Physical separation from the operatory area
- Adequate ventilation
- Autoclave and sterilization equipment
- Area for storing sterile supplies separately from non-sterile supplies

**Additional physical requirements:**
- Compliance with Ley 5.578/2016 — mandatory DEA (Automated External Defibrillator) in health establishments
- Waste management: contract with a certified company for pathological waste disposal (Resolución S.G. 750/2002)
- Water potability verification (ESSAP/ERSSAN)
- Electrical installation compliance (ANDE low voltage regulations)
- If X-ray equipment is present: ARN (Autoridad Regulatoria Radiológica y Nuclear) authorization required

**Accessibility:** Full compliance with Ley 4.934/2013 is mandatory — ramps, elevators if steps exist, accessible restrooms

### Costs
The tariff schedule is defined in **Resolución S.G. N° 106/2013** — fees for inspection, issuance of habilitation certificates, modification of category, legal representatives, and regentes. The exact amount varies by establishment size and type. Contact MSPBS directly for current fee schedule.

## 2. Business Structure Options

### S.A. (Sociedad Anónima)
- Traditional corporate structure
- Requires a minimum of 2 shareholders
- Capital divided into shares
- More complex administration, higher costs
- Suitable for larger operations or multiple partners

### S.R.L. (Sociedad de Responsabilidad Limitada)
- Limited liability company
- Requires minimum 2 partners, maximum 25
- Capital divided into quotas
- Simpler than S.A. but still requires formal structure
- Common for small to medium businesses

### E.A.S. (Empresa por Acciones Simplificada)
- Created by **Ley 6.089/2018** and its amendments
- Designed specifically for entrepreneurs and SMEs
- **Minimum 1 shareholder** (can be a single person)
- Simplified administration and accounting
- Lower setup and maintenance costs vs. S.A. or S.R.L.
- Managed through the MIC (Ministerio de Industria y Comercio) portal: **eas.mic.gov.py**
- Still must obtain RUC and comply with tax obligations

### Best Structure for a Solo Dentist Practice
**E.A.S. is the recommended structure** for a solo dentist practice (autonomous professional) because:
- Can be established by a single person
- Lower administrative burden and cost
- Flexible governance
- Register directly at eas.mic.gov.py
- After establishing the EAS, register for RUC and Timbrado

An alternative is to operate as a **persona física** (sole proprietor/professional autonomous), which is simpler but offers less legal protection and may be less credible for some commercial arrangements.

### Tax Implications

**IVA (Value Added Tax):** 10% standard rate, 5% for basic goods

**IRP (Impuesto a la Renta Personal):** Replaced IRACIS for individuals. Personal income tax rate of **10%** on local-source income for individuals. Gains from foreign sources may be exempt.

**IRE (Impuesto a la Renta Empresarial):** Applies to enterprises (S.A., S.R.L., E.A.S. as legal entities). Rate is generally **10%** on net income ( Paraguay's competitive flat rate). However, this depends on the tax category and elections made.

**IRE RESIMPLE:** Simplified regime for small businesses — option for small EAS/S.A./S.R.L.

**IRP for professionals:** Professionals operating as personas físicas (sole practitioners) pay IRP on their income. The professional must register as "responsable simple" or "responsable completo" depending on volume and type of transactions.

**Key point:** Paraguay has a favorable tax environment — 10% on income, no capital gains tax in many cases, 0% on foreign income for non-residents. A competent contador (accountant) should be engaged to determine the optimal structure and tax regime for the specific practice.

**Timbrado:** All invoices must have a valid Timbrado authorization number (from SET — Subsecretaría de Estado de Tributación). Timbrado is obtained after establishing the RUC. The Timbrado links to a specific series of invoices.

## 3. Labor Requirements

### When Hiring a Receptionist or Dental Assistant

**Code of Labor (Ley 213/93):** governs all employment relationships in Paraguay

### Employer Obligations

1. **Social Security (IPS — Instituto de Previsión Social):**
   - Employee contribution: **9%** of salary
   - Employer contribution: **16.5%** of salary
   - The employer is responsible for withholding and remitting both portions monthly
   - Coverage includes health insurance, pension, maternity leave, disability

2. **Aguinaldo (Year-End Bonus):**
   - Obligatory under Paraguayan labor law
   - Equal to one month's salary (or proportional if the employee has not worked a full year)
   - Typically paid in December (can be divided in two installments: June and December per some collective agreements)
   - Must be paid regardless of the employee's performance

3. **Labor Contract:**
   - Must be in writing for most employment relationships
   - Contract must be registered with the MTESS (Ministerio de Trabajo, Empleo y Seguridad Social)
   - Probationary period is permitted (duration must be specified)

4. **Minimum Wage:**
   - Paraguay has a national minimum wage — check current rate with MTESS
   - Salary must meet or exceed minimum wage

5. **Working Hours:**
   - Standard: 40 hours per week (8 hours/day)
   - Overtime paid at premium rates (50% extra on weekdays, 100% on rest days/holidays)
   - Weekly rest of at least 35 consecutive hours (usually Sunday + part of Saturday)

6. **Annual Leave:**
   - 12 working days per year after 1 year of service
   - Increases with seniority (14 days after 5 years, etc.)

7. **Maternity Leave:**
   - 18 weeks paid leave (shared with father in some cases under Law 5.508/2015)
   - Protected employment during leave

8. **Termination:**
   - Requires just cause or settlement agreement
   - Severance pay applies depending on termination reason and length of service
   - 30 days minimum notice (or payment in lieu)

9. **REOP (Registro de Empleadores y Obreros):**
   - Must be registered with MTESS as an employer
   - Planillas laborales (payroll records) — currently updating as per 2024 changes that eliminated mandatory annual presentation

10. **Occupational Health:**
    - Must provide a safe workplace
    - Some dental practices may require specific occupational health protocols given the nature of the work (radiation exposure for X-ray operators, chemical exposure, etc.)

**Important:** Even a part-time receptionist triggers all these obligations. Casual or "part-time" arrangements that don't comply with minimum wage and social security requirements are illegal.

## 4. RUC and Timbrado

### RUC (Registro Único del Contribuyentes)

**What it is:** The tax identification registry for all taxpayers (individuals and businesses) in Paraguay. Managed by the **Subsecretaría de Estado de Tributación (SET)** — formerly DNIT, now restructured.

**Who needs it:** Every person or entity that will issue tax documents (invoices, receipts) must have a RUC.

### Registration Steps for a Professional / Sole Practitioner

1. **Identify your tax category:**
   - **Persona física** (individual professional) — for a solo dentist
   - **Persona jurídica** (legal entity) — for S.A., S.R.L., E.A.S.

2. **Complete the pre-registration online** at: `https://servicios.set.gov.py/eset-publico/preinscripcionIService.do`
   - The system will guide through entering basic data

3. **Required documents (persona física):**
   - Photocopy of cédula de identidad (ID)
   - Proof of professional registration (Registro Profesional from MSPBS)
   - Address verification

4. **Required documents (persona jurídica):**
   - Company charter / articles of incorporation
   - Tax ID numbers of representatives
   - Company address documentation

5. **After RUC registration:**
   - Receive a RUC number (11 digits)
   - Register the economic activity (磐 — specific codes for dental services)

### Timbrado (Invoice Authorization)

**What it is:** Timbrado is the authorization number that must appear on every tax document (factura, receipt). It is linked to a specific series of documents.

**How it works:**
- The RUC holder must request Timbrado authorization from SET
- Documents are pre-printed or generated via a certified electronic system with the Timbrado
- Each Timbrado series covers a specific range of document numbers

**Process:**
1. Have an active RUC
2. Request Timbrado through the SET portal or in person
3. Receive authorization for specific document series
4. All invoices issued must carry the valid Timbrado number

**For electronic invoicing:** Paraguay has moved toward mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) for certain taxpayers. A certified invoicing system (software) must be used.

### Professional Registration (Registro Profesional)

**This is separate from the tax RUC.** For a dentist to practice legally:
- Must register with the **Dirección de Registro y Control de Profesiones en Salud** (MSPBS)
- Submit professional title (degree), cedulación, and other credentials
- Pay the relevant fees
- The registry is checked during the habilitación process

## 5. Professional Regulations

### Círculo Odontológico Paraguayo (COP)

**Website:** cop.com.py

COP is the principal professional association for dentists in Paraguay. While membership is not strictly mandatory for legal practice, COP plays an important role in:

- **Continuing education** — offers certification and recertification programs for specialties
- **Standards and quality** — maintains professional standards and promotes excellence in dental practice
- **Representation** — advocates for the professional interests of dentists in Paraguay
- **Ethical guidance** — sets ethical standards for the profession

### Regulatory Framework for Dental Practice

The exercise of odontology in Paraguay is regulated by **Decreto 22.439/98**, which establishes:

- Only persons with a proper dental degree and registered with MSPBS may practice odontology
- Foreign titles must be revalidated/homologated through the Ministry of Education and Sciences (MEC)
- Continuing education may be required for specialty registrations

### Specialties

The **Resolución S.G. N° 316/2023** approves the catalog of clinical specialties for odontology career at MSPBS. Dentists who claim a specialty must be registered as a specialist in the Dirección de Registro y Control de Profesiones en Salud.

### When Is COP Membership Relevant?

- Some insurance providers or institutions may require COP membership
- Referral networks and professional credibility
- Access to COP continuing education programs and certifications
- Professional liability insurance may be easier to obtain through COP

### Other Relevant Professional Bodies

- **Sociedad Paraguaya de Odontopediatría** — for pediatric dentistry
- Specialty societies exist for endodontics, orthodontics, implantology, etc.

## 6. Patient Data Rights

### Relevant Law

**Ley Nº 7.593/2025** — "De Protección de Datos Personales en la República del Paraguay"
Promulgated: November 27, 2025
Status: In force as of 2025

### Key Provisions for Healthcare / Dental Practices

**Data classification:** Health data (including dental records) is classified as **datos personales sensibles** (sensitive personal data) under Article 3 of the law. The law explicitly defines health data as sensitive.

**Treatment of sensitive data:** Article 20 prohibits treatment of sensitive data EXCEPT in specific cases, including:
- When the data subject (patient) has given explicit consent
- When necessary for preventive medicine, diagnosis, medical or surgical treatment
- When necessary for the management of health or social assistance systems, under conditions of confidentiality

**Confidentiality obligation:** Article 4(10) establishes a principle of confidentiality that persists even after the professional relationship ends.

**Patient rights:** Under Article 26, patients (data subjects) have the right to:
- **Access** their personal data
- **Rectification** of inaccurate data
- **Deletion** (supresión) of data when no longer necessary
- **Portability** — receive their data in a structured format
- **Object** to certain processing

**Habeas Data action:** Article 26 provides for habeas data as a judicial remedy if the professional does not honor the patient's rights requests within 30 days.

**Data minimization:** Only data strictly necessary for the treatment purpose should be collected and retained.

**Security measures:** Article 16 requires appropriate technical and organizational security measures. Dental practices must implement measures to prevent data loss, unauthorized access, or alteration.

**Breach notification:** Article 17 requires notification to the data protection authority and affected patients within 72 hours of discovering a data security incident.

### Can a Dentist Take Their Patient Database When Leaving a Clinic?

**The answer is nuanced and requires careful legal analysis:**

1. **Patient records belong to the practice/institution** — the physical and digital records are typically the property of whoever funded and maintains them. However, the data belongs to the patient.

2. **The patient has the right to access their records** — under Ley 7.593/2025, patients can request a copy of all their personal data. This includes dental history, X-rays, treatment plans.

3. **The departing dentist can take anonymized/aggregate data only** — if the data is anonymized (no patient identifiers), it may be used for professional purposes, research, or continuity of care. However, identifiable patient data cannot simply be taken.

4. **Continuity of care exception** — when a dentist leaves a clinic, they may retain minimal patient data necessary to continue treating those patients who choose to follow them, under the "legitimate interest" or "health protection" bases in Article 5(7).

5. **Non-compete and employment agreements** — if the dentist was employed by a clinic, the employment contract may have clauses restricting solicitation of patients or带走 patient data. Such clauses must be evaluated for enforceability.

**Practical recommendation:** 
- Never拿走完整的patient database with identifiable information
- If leaving to open own practice, patients should be notified and given the choice to have their records transferred
- Maintain proper documentation and patient consent forms that specify data handling policies
- Consult with a Paraguayan attorney specializing in health law for specific situations

### Additional Protections

**Professional secrecy:** Dentists have an ethical and legal duty of confidentiality (secreto profesional) beyond data protection laws. Violation can result in professional sanctions from COP and civil/criminal liability.

**Clinical history:** The dental clinical history belongs to the patient. The patient has the right to receive a copy of their complete dental history, including images (X-rays, photos).

## Summary: Priority Checklist for Opening a Consultorio

1. **Professional registration** with MSPBS Dirección de Registro y Control de Profesiones en Salud
2. **Register business** — recommended: E.A.S. (single shareholder, lower cost)
3. **Obtain RUC** from SET (online pre-registration)
4. **Request Timbrado** for invoicing (linked to RUC)
5. **Engage a contador** for IRP/IVA obligations
6. **Find a location** that meets physical requirements (9m²+ operatory, bathroom with accessibility, sterilization area)
7. **Prepare architectural plans** (scale 1:50) and submit for habilitación
8. **Pass inspection** by MSPBS Dirección de Establecimientos de Salud
9. **Obtain Habilitación Certificate** before opening
10. **Register with MTESS as employer** (even for 1 part-time receptionist)
11. **Set up IPS social security** for any employees
12. **Implement patient data protection** (Lei 7.593/2025 compliance — consent forms, data security, breach notification procedure)
13. **Consider COP membership** for professional credibility and continuing education
