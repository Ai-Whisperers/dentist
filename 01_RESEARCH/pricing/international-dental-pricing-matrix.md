| Factor | Paraguay | Impact |
|--------|----------|--------|

- **Lower specialist density** — fewer endodontists, periodontists = more GP scope
- **No nursing/assistant unions** — flexible hiring
- **Education cost** — university training cheaper, no $300k dental school debt
- **Exchange rate arbitrage** — USD-denominated savings on imported materials

1. **Mexico (Cancun, Tijuana, Los Algodones)** — 1M+ US patients/year
2. **Costa Rica** — 50,000+ medical tourists/year, dental major component
3. **Thailand** — Well-established medical tourism infrastructure

6. **Colombia (Medellín, Bogotá)** — Rising star, 50%+ cost vs. USA

- Mexico: US patients cross border for 50–70% savings
- Costa Rica: US/Canadian patients fly in for major work

## PART 5: FAIR PRICING PHILOSOPHY

### 5.1 What Makes Pricing "Ethical" vs "Extortionate"

**Extortionate pricing indicators:**
- 2–3x the cost of equivalent-quality regional alternatives without justification
- Charging US prices to patients who earn local wages
- Pushing unnecessary procedures to increase ticket size
- Price opacity — hiding costs until treatment is underway
- Exploiting dental tourism desperation (patients from countries where they can't afford care)

**Fair pricing principles:**

1. **Cost-plus with reasonable margin**
   - Materials + lab costs + time + overhead + reasonable profit
   - Reasonable margin for a solo practitioner: 30–45% on procedures
   - Do not charge "what the market will bear" — charge what reflects your actual costs + value

2. **Geographic arbitrage transparency**
   - If you're pricing 50% below USA, acknowledge that this is legitimate — cost structures are different
   - Don't pretend you're offering "US quality at Paraguayan prices" without the infrastructure to back it up
   - Do advertise the savings honestly: "Premium dental care at 50–60% below US prices"

3. **Value-based pricing for planning services**
   - Your expertise in diagnosis and planning has value
   - Charge for planning sessions separately — this is ethical because patients pay for expertise, not just materials
   - Don't bundle planning into procedure costs and then charge procedure prices

4. **No unnecessary upselling**
   - If a filling will do, don't recommend a crown
   - If a crown will do, don't recommend an implant
   - "No overtreatment" philosophy IS the ethical differentiator
   - Document your conservative recommendations — they build trust

5. **Payment accessibility**
   - Offer Pagopar/Bancard installments for high-value cases
   - Financing enables patients to afford care they need without paying full cash upfront
   - Interest-free or low-interest payment plans are ethical if clearly disclosed

6. **Second opinions are priced fairly**
   - A second opinion is worth paying for — it saves patients from unnecessary treatment
   - Price second opinions at a level that respects your time but doesn't gatekeep access
   - Gs 400,000–$600,000 for second opinion consult is fair (takes 45–60 min, requires documentation)

### 5.2 Lab Costs vs. Material Costs — Price Breakdown

**For a typical zirconia crown in Paraguay:**

| Component | Cost (USD) | % of Price |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Dentist time** (preparation + placement, ~90 min) | $50–80 | 12–18% |
| **Lab cost** (zirconia framework + ceramic layering) | $120–200 | 28–35% |
| **Materials** (impression materials, temps, cement) | $30–50 | 8–12% |
| **Overhead** (rent, utilities, equipment amort.) | $60–100 | 15–22% |
| **Margin** (before tax, reinvestment) | $100–150 | 22–30% |
| **Total** | **$360–$580** | 100% |

**vs. USA equivalent:**

| Component | Cost (USD) | % of Price |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Dentist time** | $150–250 | 15–20% |
| **Lab cost** (same quality, higher US lab overhead) | $400–700 | 35–45% |
| **Materials** | $50–80 | 5–8% |
| **Overhead** (rent, admin, insurance) | $200–400 | 20–30% |
| **Margin** | $150–300 | 15–25% |
| **Total** | **$1,200–$2,200** | 100% |

**Key insight:** Lab costs are the largest single variable in crown pricing. Paraguay's lower lab costs (local labs vs. US labs) are the structural advantage. A Paraguayan lab charging $150 for a crown that a US lab charges $500 does NOT mean the Paraguayan lab is inferior — the cost difference is overhead and market structure, not quality.

### 5.3 Premium vs. Exploitative — The Line

**Premium pricing is ethical when:**
- It reflects actual cost of quality materials + lab work
- It includes fair compensation for expertise (not just time)
- It enables investment in better equipment, training, materials
- It is transparent — patient knows full cost before treatment
- It includes follow-up and warranty support
- It doesn't exploit patients who have no alternative

**Exploitative pricing is when:**
- Cost is 3–4x regional equivalent with no justification
- Patient is charged US rates but local cost structure
- Treatment is recommended without clinical necessity to increase revenue
- Patient is not given options (informed consent includes financial options)
- Price is revealed only after treatment is started

**For Dra. GGP's positioning:**
- Premium prices at 50–60% below US for equivalent quality = ethical
- Prices at same level as Buenos Aires for similar quality = fair
- Prices 30–40% below Buenos Aires for similar or better quality = excellent value
- Prices should NEVER match US levels — that would be exploitative given cost structure
