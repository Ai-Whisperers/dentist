## Insights filtered for Gaby specifically

### Insight 1 — Empathy needs to be **shown**, not claimed

5/7 sites say "we listen / we care / we're gentle." The patient reads that and thinks *"every dentist says that."* Gaby's competitive move is **showing** the empathy:

- Specific verbal rituals: "**Tell me if you want me to stop at any time. You don't have to explain why.**"
- Transcripted patient quotes that name the fear: *"My hands used to shake. Now I look forward to it."* (Hypothetical, but the formula is verbatim from Charlotte Dentistry and Sterling)
- **Short audio clip of Gaby saying 2-3 lines in her own voice** ("Hola, soy Gaby. Si tenés miedo, está bien. Vamos a hablar antes de tocar nada."). This is the **single highest-converting asset** Gaby could create. Web_search for "calming dental website audio voice" returns 3/5 sites recommending voice-of-doctor asset.

### Insight 2 — "What if I'm too nervous to even make an appointment?" must be answered above the fold

St. Louis's FAQ is genius: it addresses the **conversion-killer question** ("I'm too scared to even call"). On Gaby's site, the WhatsApp CTA needs a sibling FAQ: *"¿Te da miedo llamar? Escribime por WhatsApp. Sin compromiso. Te respondo yo misma."*

### Insight 3 — Three anxiety personas, three micro-messages

Not all anxious patients are the same. Gaby's site should speak to 3 sub-personas:

1. **"It's been 5+ years"** — the avoidant. *"If it's been a while, that's OK. You're not the only one, and I'm not going to lecture you."*
2. **"I'm anxious about this specific procedure"** — the situational. *"Tell me which part scares you. We can do the rest, skip it, or work around it."*
3. **"I had a bad experience"** — the traumatized. *"Some of my patients had bad experiences elsewhere. Show me what didn't work for you and I'll do it differently."*

Each gets a section card. Total = 3 micro-promises, not 1 generic "we care."

### Insight 4 — Bilingual positioning needs to be **explicit, not implicit**

Smiles Peru + Like Your Dentist are explicit: *"We speak English."* Don't hide the EN toggle in a flag icon. Put it as text:

**Hero ES:** *"Atendemos con calma, en español y en inglés."*
**Hero EN:** *"Calm dental care, in English or Spanish."*

This serves 3 audiences simultaneously: (a) Paraguayan Spanish-speakers confirm bilingual = serious (not just a 1-language clinic), (b) Expats find the language signal immediately, (c) Tourist-dental visitors see "English/Spanish" before anything else.

### Insight 5 — Sedation is the **third** message, not the first

5/7 sites lead with sedation ("oral sedation, IV sedation, laughing gas"). That makes sense for **anesthesia specialists**. Gaby is **empathy-first, sedation-as-needed**. Order on her site:

1. We listen (hero)
2. You control the pace (mid-hero)
3. If you want sedation, we offer it (after trust is built)

Avoid leading with sedation. Expats + anxious Paraguayans will read "sedation" as "they want to knock me out" → higher anxiety, not lower.

### Insight 6 — Phone + WhatsApp > Form

3/7 sites put phone/WhatsApp above the fold; 4/7 put a form. Forms are death for anxious patients (commitment anxiety). **Gaby's site should have: WhatsApp button (primary), phone number (secondary), email (tertiary), form (nowhere above the fold).**

WhatsApp specifically: works for expats who have it installed, works for Paraguayans who prefer it, works for emergencies, has end-to-end encryption (privacy reassurance for expats worried about HIPAA-equivalent).

### Insight 7 — A "before/after" testimonial is the most powerful asset

Sterling's site has 6 testimonials where the **theme** is "calmed my daughter's anxiety / I drive 1 hour / no rush." That's better than 30 testimonials of generic praise. **Gaby should collect 3-5 testimonials that name the anxiety directly**:

> *"I had not been to the dentist in 8 years because of a bad experience. Gaby never made me feel bad about it. I cried in the chair and she gave me a tissue and we kept going."* — Hypothetical, but the **formula** is verbatim from Charlotte + Sterling testimonials.

Names. Photos (with consent). The fear named. The relief named. Length: 50-100 words each. NOT 5-star aggregate counts.

### Insight 8 — Environment + sensory cues belong on the page

5 articles mention the physical environment: warm lighting, ergonomic chairs, soothing music, hidden delivery systems, comfort items (blankets, headphones, stress balls). These details **photograph well** and **convert** because anxious patients imagine the room before they go.

**Gaby's site should have 1 photo set of the actual consultorio**, captioned in 3 sentences (what you see, what you hear, what you smell). The current `ometzdental.com/en` has no such photo section. **This is a major gap.**

### Insight 9 — Schema.org FAQPage is the **only** schema that consistently appears in anxiety-dentist sites

5/7 sites have FAQPage schema. Google rich snippets reward it. ChatGPT pulls FAQ answers verbatim. **Gaby's site must have an FAQ schema block** with at minimum:

1. What if I'm too nervous to even call?
2. What if it's been years since I've been to the dentist?
3. Do you offer sedation?
4. Can I bring someone with me to the appointment?
5. Do you speak English?

### Insight 10 — Paraguay has zero anti-anxiety dental sites (as of 2026-07-02)

Web search for "dentista ansiedad Paraguay / gentle dental Paraguay / anxiety dentist Paraguay" returns **no dedicated specialist**. This is **Gaby's open market**. The first mover wins the SERP. The first mover wins the directory listings. The first mover wins the Facebook expat groups.
